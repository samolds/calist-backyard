## Layout and flow (feng shui)

**Verdict: a good place to hang out.** The layout already follows the main feng shui ideas: a protected seat with a
view, an open space in front of it, and utility things kept far away. A few small additions (below) would make it
better.

**What works**
- **The "commanding position":** the pergola sits against the back wall of the house, so you sit with a solid wall
  behind you and look out over the whole yard toward the parking pad and alley. You can see anyone arriving without turning
  around. In feng shui and in everyday terms, this is the most relaxing place to sit.
- **An open "bright hall" in front of the seat:** the lawn in the middle stays open (about 615 sq ft). Feng shui
  favors open, uncluttered space in front of the main seat; practically, it's room for the dog and a view.
- **Utility at the far end:** the parking pad and the compost tumbler are at the alley end, about 25' from the
  pergola, so neither smell nor clutter reaches the seating.
- **Shade where the sun is strongest:** afternoon sun comes from the southwest (the right side). The shade sail is on
  that side of the pergola, and the shrubs line that edge. The tree shades the upper lawn and planters; it is too far
  from the pergola to shade it much.
- **Soft edges:** the curved grass notch by the half wall and the rounded lawn corner keep the yard from feeling like
  a grid of rectangles.
- **Wood and living plants dominate:** the pergola, the tree, the planters and the lawn give the yard a calm, green
  feel.

**What could be better**
- **The path is a straight line.** Arriving from the parking pad, the natural route is a straight shot down the lawn
  to the steps. Feng shui calls a long straight path "rushing chi"; it also wears a bare stripe in the grass.
  A gently curving stepping-stone path, from the pad past the planters to the pergola's front-left, slows the walk
  down and protects the lawn. Lay the stones before the new lawn goes in (Part C).
- **The A/C unit is just past the pergola's back-right corner.** Its noise and hot air will bother anyone sitting on
  that side. Put the lounge seating or hammock in the **left bay** of the pergola, and use the right bay (where the
  table is) for dining. A lattice or planter screen can hide the unit; keep the clearance its manual calls for
  (often about 2').
- **There's no water element.** The yard has wood, earth and metal, but no water. A birdbath or a small recirculating
  fountain, visible from the pergola, adds movement, sound that masks the A/C and street, and draws birds. The
  open area near the planters or the lawn's lower-left corner works.
- **Evening light.** String lights or solar lights along the pergola beams make it usable after dark and give the
  yard a warm focal point at night (the "fire" element).
- **Privacy on the southwest side.** The pergola's right edge is about 11" from the lot line. Taller shrubs in the
  mulch strip, or a removable outdoor curtain on the pergola's right end, add enclosure. A permanent wall on the
  pergola would need to be in the permit and Landmark drawings, so a curtain is simpler.

## The neighbor's white fence (glare and heat)

The white vinyl fence runs along the whole left (northeast) lot line, right at the outer edge of the 3' 5" sidewalk.
Its yard side faces **southwest**, so it's in full sun from late morning to sunset, the hottest part of the day.

**What it's actually doing:** white vinyl reflects most of the sunlight that hits it (it stays cool itself). That
light bounces back across the sidewalk, the planters and the lawn edge. The effect is strongest within a few feet of the
fence: glare, hotter plants in the planters, and a hot walk on the sidewalk. It raises the air temperature of the whole
yard only a little; the bigger heat sources are the concrete pad, patio and sidewalk, which soak up sun and give it
back into the evening. **Don't try to darken the fence** (it isn't yours, and a dark surface would get hot and radiate
heat instead of reflecting it).

**The fix: catch the sun before it reaches the fence, with plants.** Leaves absorb the light and cool the air by
transpiring; nothing else does both. Everything below stands on your side and never touches the neighbor's fence.

1. **Trellises on the three raised planters (main fix).** The planters sit about 3' 8" in front of the fence. Fix a
   5–6' trellis along each planter's fence side and grow a vine on it. That gives about 18' of green screen exactly
   where the planters and the lawn edge get the glare.
2. **Trellis planter boxes on the sidewalk (optional, to extend it).** Planter boxes with built-in trellises,
   about 12" deep, set against the fence on the sidewalk, leave a 2' 5" walkway. No digging and nothing attached.
3. **A fast first-summer fix:** vines take a season or two to fill in. Until then, zip-tie a **natural reed or
   bamboo screen roll**, or **70–90% shade cloth** in dark green or brown, to the trellises.
4. **Tall grasses in the planters** (for the planters' fence-side edge): 'Karl Foerster' feather reed grass (5' tall,
   Plant Select favorite) or native switchgrass. They soften the glare all summer and stand through the winter.

| Vine (on the trellis) | Why | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| **Western white clematis** (*Clematis ligusticifolia*) **(recommended)** | Colorado native; fast twiner; white flowers in late summer, then fluffy seed heads; low water once established | Vigorous; trim it so it stays on your trellis |
| Native hops (*Humulus lupulus* var. *neomexicanus*) | Colorado native and very fast; dies back each winter, so it shades only in summer, when you need it | Spreads by roots in open ground; a raised planter contains it |
| Trumpet honeysuckle 'Major Wheeler' (*Lonicera sempervirens*) | Red tubular flowers for hummingbirds; not invasive (unlike Japanese honeysuckle) | Needs moderate water |

**Avoid:** Virginia creeper and Engelmann ivy (their sticky pads would climb onto and mark the neighbor's vinyl),
trumpet creeper (suckers everywhere), English ivy and silver lace vine (invasive).

- **☐ ACTION:** Tell the neighbor what you're planning. If they're happy for you to attach vine wires to their fence,
  you can skip the separate trellises (screws can void a vinyl fence's warranty, so let them decide).
- **☐ ACTION:** Ask Landmark Preservation (or CPD in Step B2.3) whether a freestanding trellis or screen needs review in
  the historic district. Plants never do.

# Part A: Plant the tree (do this first)

**Goal:** a tree in the ground by **Oct 31, 2026**. No permit is needed for a private yard tree, and Landmark
Preservation doesn't review plantings. Steps A1 (plumber) and A3 (811) need a few business days' notice, so book
them right away; you can choose the tree (A2) while you wait.

## A1. Locate the sewer line (required before you dig)
Your private sewer line runs from the house to the city main in the alley. It isn't on any city map and probably
crosses the yard, possibly near the tree spot. Tree roots get into old or cracked sewer pipes, and **811 (Step A3)
does not mark private sewer lines**.

- **☐ ACTION:** Book a plumber for a **sewer camera inspection with locating** from the house cleanout. Ask them to:
  - **paint the line's route on the grass** all the way to the alley;
  - note its depth at the tree spot;
  - report the pipe material and whether roots are already in it (fix any problem before planting);
  - **for Part B, while they're there:** note the depth near the four pergola pier spots (front-left, front-middle,
    front-right and back-right posts). One visit then covers both projects.
- **☐ ACTION:** Measure from the painted line to the tree spot. The spot (purple in the diagram) is
  **17' 11" from the left (NE) boundary** and **9' 4" into the grass from the parking pad's edge**.
  - Common arborist guidance: keep the trunk **at least 10'** from a sewer line, and **15–20'** for the large shade
    trees in A2.1.
  - If the line runs closer than that, move the spot away from the line or pick a smaller tree (A2.2) or an
    evergreen (A2.3).

## A2. Choose the tree
The spot is about 18' from the left (NE) lot line, **about 7–8' from the right (SW) lot line**, about 27' from the
alley, and in full sun. Because it's that close to the right line, a **25' canopy reaches about 5' over the
neighbor's side** (the lighter purple circle in the diagram), and the 25–35' shade trees in A2.1 would reach 5–10'
over. A **smaller tree, 25' canopy or less** (A2.2), or a narrow **evergreen** (A2.3) fits the spot better and leaves
more sun for the grass and the planters, which sit 12–14' north of the spot. The chart in A2.4 shows

Every option is drought tolerant once established (the tree lilac only moderately), and none is on the

### A2.6 Your soil (clay)
**How likely is clay?** Fairly likely, at least in part. The USDA soil survey maps this part of central Denver as
*Urban land–Elyria–Ascalon complex*: mostly disturbed or filled ground over the original soils. Those soils are
sandy loam on top, over **sandy clay loam** subsoil (about 20–30% clay), with pockets of **clay loam and clay**
(the Weld and Racas soils in the same map unit). The pH is **alkaline (about 7.4–8.4)**. After 100+ years of city
use, expect compacted, heavy soil that drains slowly, even where it isn't pure clay. Step C1 explains how to test it.

The **Heavy clay** column in the A2.1–A2.3 tables rates each tree for this kind of heavy, alkaline Front Range soil
(general nursery guidance).

**Drainage test before you plant (5 minutes, then wait):** once the hole is dug, fill it with water, let it drain,
then fill it again. If water is still standing the next day, drainage is poor. Choose a "Good" or "Very good" tree
and set the root flare 2–3" above grade.

- **☐ ACTION:** Pick a first choice and a backup, using the sewer-line distance from A1 and the clay ratings in the tables.

## A5. Plant it (by Oct 31)
1. **☐ ACTION:** Water the root ball well the day before.
2. **☐ ACTION:** Dig the hole **2–3× as wide** as the root ball and **no deeper than the root ball**, so the root flare
   sits at grade (1–2" above grade in clay). Rough up the sides of the hole; in clay, smooth sides act like a bathtub.
   Do the drainage test in A2.6.
3. **☐ ACTION:** Remove the pot. For balled-and-burlapped trees, set the tree in the hole, then cut away the wire
   basket and burlap from the top and sides. Loosen any circling roots.
4. **☐ ACTION:** Backfill with the soil you dug out (no amendments), firming it gently. Water slowly until the hole
   is soaked.
5. **☐ ACTION:** Spread **3" of wood mulch** in a 3–4' ring (6–8' for a piñon), kept a few inches off the trunk.
6. Stake only if the tree leans or the site is windy; remove stakes after one year.
7. **☐ ACTION:** Wrap thin-barked trees (maples, lilac, hawthorn) from November to April for their first winters, to
   prevent sunscald.

- **☐ ACTION:** Water deeply **monthly from November to March** on days above 40°F with no snow on the ground.
- First summer: a deep soak about once a week unless the lawn sprinklers already cover it (never for a piñon).
- Don't prune the first year except for broken branches.

# Part C: New lawn (after the tree and pergola are established)

**When:** after the pergola is built, so the crew and materials don't trample new grass. Plant DOG TUFF™ plugs
**late May to July**; lay fescue sod in **spring or mid-August to mid-September**. The grass area is about
**615 sq ft** (about 580 sq ft after the planters).

**The goal:** soft grass your dog can roll in, that holds up to a dog, grows in heavy clay, **survives Denver winters
(Zone 5b/6a, dry, with hard freezes) and doesn't need much water**. Browning in winter is fine.

## C1. Test the soil
- **☐ ACTION:** Send a sample to the **CSU Soil, Water and Plant Testing Lab**: https://agsci.colostate.edu/soiltestinglab/
  Ask for the routine test plus texture. It tells you how much clay you have, the pH, salts and what fertilizer you need.
- Quick home check: shake a cup of soil in a jar of water and let it settle for a day. Sand drops first, then silt;
  clay settles last. A clay layer more than about a third of the total means heavy clay.

## C2. Choose the grass

| Grass | Denver winter | Water once established | For a dog | Clay | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **DOG TUFF™ grass** (Plant Select) **(recommended)** | Hardy to Zone 5 and 7,500 ft. Goes **tan from mid-fall to late spring**, then greens up | **Low** ("moderate to xeric") | Bred for dogs: tolerates urine and heavy wear; soft and cushioned | Rated for clay | Sold as plugs, not sod; takes most of the first summer to fill in. Weed-resistant once established |

## C3. Prepare the clay soil
CSU's guidance for Colorado soils: the most important thing to add is **organic matter** (compost), not fertilizer.
1. **☐ ACTION:** Remove the old grass and weeds (rent a sod cutter, or kill it and scrape it off).
2. **☐ ACTION:** Fix the grade so water drains **away from the house**. Check that the sprinklers cover the whole area.
3. **☐ ACTION:** Spread **compost at 3–4 cubic yards per 1,000 sq ft** (CSU). For this yard that's about
   **2½ cubic yards**, roughly a 1½" layer. Buy screened compost with low salts from a landscape supply yard.
4. **☐ ACTION:** Till it into the **top 6–8"** while the soil is just moist (not wet, or the clay forms hard clods).
   - **Stay 5'+ from the new tree's trunk:** only spread compost there, don't till, or you'll cut its roots.
   - The sewer line from A1 is far deeper than the tiller goes.
5. **☐ ACTION:** Rake it level, remove clods and stones, roll it lightly, and water it to settle. Fill any low spots.
6. **☐ ACTION:** If you're adding the curving stepping-stone path (see "Layout and flow"), set the stones now, before
   planting, so the grass grows in around them.
- **Don't add sand** to clay; it can set up like concrete. **Gypsum** doesn't loosen Colorado clay either. Compost does.

## C4. Plant it
- **DOG TUFF™ plugs (recommended):** plant late May to July, about 12" apart, and water as the grower directs until
  they knit together. Planting by July gives them a full summer to root in before their first winter.
  **Keep the dog off** until the plugs have filled in.
- **Sod (fescue option):** moisten the soil 6–8" deep a few days before delivery. Lay it the day it arrives, with
  staggered seams pushed tight. Roll it, then keep it moist (not soggy) until it roots in, about 2–3 weeks: tug a
  corner to check (CSU). **Keep the dog off** for about 3 weeks.

## C5. Keep it healthy (yearly)
- Mow at **2½–3"** and leave the clippings (CSU). DOG TUFF™ only needs mowing late May through August (Plant
  Select). Water deeply and less often; on clay, split each watering into two runs an hour apart so it soaks in
  instead of running off.
- Rinse fresh dog-pee spots with a hose to prevent yellow patches.
- **☐ ACTION: Core-aerate once a year** (spring or early fall) starting the year after planting. CSU calls core
  aeration "essential" for high-traffic lawns; it relieves clay compaction. Water the day before; aerate in two
  directions; leave the plugs on the lawn to break down.

# Appendix: picturing the finished yard (AI image from a photo)

**In progress (paused).** The idea: take a photo of the yard today and have a generative image model draw the plan
into it (pergola, sail, tree, planters, fence screen, ground cover). The tools and a log of every try are in
`explorations/ai_renders/` (`LOG.md`).

**It runs fully offline on this Mac** (Apple M2 Pro, 16 GB of memory). A script edits one part of the photo at a time
with **FLUX.2 [klein] 4B**, an open (Apache-2.0) image-editing model, run locally through
[mflux](https://github.com/mflux-community/mflux). Each edit takes about **1 minute** and uses up to about 8 GB of
memory; plan on several tries per element. The model is a one-time ~16 GB download, shared with other projects on
this Mac.

**How to get the most accurate picture**
- Add **one element at a time** (pergola, then sail, then tree, planters, fence screen, ground cover), each time
  painting over only the area where it goes.
- For better placement, the assistant can draw guide outlines from the plan's measurements over the photo's
  viewpoint, and the model fills them in.
- Use **one wide photo** taken from the patio or from the parking pad, in daylight.

**Limits**
- The images **won't be to scale**, and the model may invent details. Use them to judge the look and feel; the plan
  drawings stay the reference for sizes and positions.

**Privacy**
- Keep the photo and the results in `private/`, so `publish.sh` never puts them on the public branch.
- Strip the photo's location data (GPS) before saving it to the repo.

- **☐ ACTION (optional, any time):** Ask the assistant to resume the renders (next steps are in `LOG.md`).
